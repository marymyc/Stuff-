# Style guide (distilled from two reference videos, visual analysis only)

Measured/observed on two 9:16 shorts (720x1280, 30 fps, ~65-67 s). Timings are approximate (+/- 1 s).

## Look
- **Hybrid**: 2D hand-drawn characters (big white round head, thick black outline, dot eyes, noodle legs, coloured vest, one hat per role) inside a stylised 3D game-engine world (grass, stone, canyon bands, volumetric light, depth of field).
- Characters appear flat and face the camera like billboards; they scale and tilt with perspective. Expressions are drawn faces swapped per beat (neutral, angry, rage = purple head + steam, happy, shocked, worried + sweat drop, smug).
- Colour-code roles: hero teal/striped cap, rival yellow/bowler, extras green/grey helmets.
- Palettes: bright saturated day (cyan sky, green fields); cold blue/purple night with warm lanterns; orange/red/magenta canyon bands; golden hour at the payoff.

## Camera
- Virtual 3D camera moving in almost every shot: aerial fly-forward establishing shots, low ground-level shots, orbit with shallow depth of field, fast push-ins, macro slides over mechanisms, occasional dutch tilt at the climax.
- Few hard cuts; motion hides most cuts. Cross-dissolves only for time jumps (day/night/morning).

## Pacing
- No intro: first frame is already the hook. Average shot ~2-3 s (about 6-9 words).
- Arc: hook -> setup -> problem -> escalation -> visual payoff (explosion / collapse / perfect-then-fail).

## Captions
- One or two words at a time, ~0.3-0.6 s each, heavy white sans-serif, thick black outline, centred horizontally at ~70% of the height, same position all video, small pop-in scale, keyword highlight in yellow (numbers, "never", "only", ...).

## Sound
- Narration + simple SFX hits (whoosh on aerial moves, boom on explosions, rumble on collapse, spark/hiss on fuses, ding on success). SFX here are procedural (no licensing).
