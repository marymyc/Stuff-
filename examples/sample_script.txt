In 1869, two railroad companies were racing across the desert, and the first line to finish would get the whole country's money. The workers were exhausted, but the owner refused to slow down. Then a young engineer noticed something nobody else saw. Every rail had a tiny gap, only a few millimeters wide. The owner laughed. Perfect rails, he said, are the only rails that matter. But summer came, and the sun heated the steel. Every rail grew longer, pushing against its neighbor, and the track began to twist like a snake. The first train that crossed the bridge never made it to the other side. Boom.
